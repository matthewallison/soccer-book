# The High-Level Player Sheet

This is the short version. Know the four mantras by memory. Learn the big principles over time, and come back to this chapter as a quick reminder. The chapters that follow explain the ideas behind it.

Our four team mantras.

First, the practice mantra. Say it with me. [pause] [slows down] Scan. Talk. Intention. Faster.

Scan means collecting information before you need it. Talk means giving teammates useful information they cannot see. Intention means every action should have a reason. And faster means pushing the speed at which the first three can still be done correctly.

Next, the game mantra. Say it with me. [pause] [slows down] Calm mind. Fast soccer.

Here's a question. [pause] Does fast soccer mean getting rid of the ball quickly? [long pause] No. Fast soccer does not mean panic. It means seeing early, deciding quickly, and executing with control. It can mean holding the ball an extra second because you already scanned. It can mean playing backward. It can mean carrying fifteen yards because nobody stepped to you.

After mistakes, the next-play mantra. Say it with me. [pause] [slows down] Respond. Reflect. Learn. Next play.

Creative play and risk-taking will create mistakes. Mistakes should be learning moments, never reasons for shame or embarrassment.

And in pressure moments, the pressure mantra. Say it with me. [pause] [slows down] Want the moment.

Nerves are normal. They are your body getting ready. Step forward anyway.

How we want to play.

Say our team standard with me. [pause] [slows down] No hiding. No watching. Influence the game.

At every moment, ask yourself: Where could I go right now that would make me useful?

Think back to your last game. [pause] Picture one moment when you were watching instead of moving. [long pause] Where could you have gone that would have made you useful? [long pause]

When we have the ball, everyone is a potential outlet. If you're not an option, you're making the ball carrier's problem harder. Move on the pass, and move again after you pass. Use width, depth, different heights, and good spacing. Strong side attracts. Weak side prepares. Look diagonal, sideways, backward, and forward. Progress when we have an advantage, and keep the ball when we don't. And show one thing and do another: look like you're changing your mind.

When the field opens. Rondos teach us how to survive when the game gets small. But soccer also asks us to recognize when the game becomes big. When we escape pressure, the problem has changed, so look up. Don't keep playing five-yard passes in the same crowded area after the field opens. Carry into open grass, switch the field, find a runner, or attack before the defense recovers. A longer pass with a clear purpose is not a clearance. Find the biggest useful space.

Say it with me. [pause] [slows down] Small space: combine. Big space: exploit.

The goal is not to pass short or to pass long. The goal is to recognize the space and use the right solution.

Without the ball and in transition, protect dangerous space first, and get tighter as the ball and the danger get closer. Protect the middle, and delay when you need to. When we lose the ball, counterpress if we can win it back; otherwise, recover into shape immediately. Defend from a position you can attack from. And head the ball with purpose, not habit.

Brave soccer.

We want players who turn, carry, dribble, disguise passes, play through pressure, combine, and try creative solutions when they recognize the opportunity.

We will correct careless decisions, but we will not punish intelligent ideas simply because they did not work. Careless means ignoring the picture in front of you, or ignoring what we've worked on in training.

Think of a brave idea you tried that didn't work. [long pause] Were you ignoring the picture in front of you? Or was it an intelligent idea that just didn't come off? [long pause]

In the game: respond, then play the next action. Later, at a break, after the game, or on film, you'll reflect with five questions. We'll try those together in the next chapter.

Pressure moments.

The goal is not to eliminate fear. The goal is to stop fear from making the decision. At this age, the real loss is not missing the penalty. The real loss is avoiding the chance to learn how you respond under pressure.

We should fear wasting developmental opportunities more than we fear temporary failure.

You may be nervous. You may miss. Neither is shameful. But wanting the moment never means playing through pain or hiding an injury. If something hurts, tell a parent and your coach.

Compete to win, develop for the long term.

Winning is not the primary measure of development, but competing to win is part of development. So: want to win. Play for the next action, not the scoreboard.

Everyone leads.

Leadership belongs to every player, not only captains. And not every leader is loud. See what the team needs, and help provide it.

Coach yourself.

Be coachable, and learn to coach yourself. In games, self-coaching is a loop: scan, understand, decide, execute, reflect, adjust.

And after every practice, ask: What soccer problem did today's practice teach me to solve? [pause] What was the answer from your last practice? [long pause]

Team development culture.

Your effort is part of your teammate's training. We compete hard against each other to make each other better, not to rank each other.

Help each other learn. Don't grade each other. That means: describe, don't judge, and ask before giving advice. Use shared team language, and praise useful behavior specifically. Support teammates after mistakes. Body language counts, so no sighs and no eye-rolls. In games, give information and encouragement, and save advice for later. And leave selection, discipline, and major corrections to the coach.

Development ownership.

Know three things: one current strength to build on, one current development priority you chose with your coach, and what progress would look like. These will change as you improve.

Can you name all three right now? [pause] Your strength. [long pause] Your priority. [long pause] And what progress would look like. [long pause] If any of those were hard to name, that's a great conversation to have with your coach.

Your team role is where you help the team today. Your development role is the complete player you are becoming.

Want a different role or more minutes? Ask the coach yourself: What would I need to show to earn that? Talk with the coach during office hours at the field, or send a film question through the team app, with a parent included.
