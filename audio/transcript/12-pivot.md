# Pivot: Numbers Six and Eight

You're playing pivot today, one of our two deeper midfielders. The six or the eight. Let's get your head there before you get to the field.

If you're somewhere you can, close your eyes.

Your job is to connect the team, protect the space underneath the ball, and help control the tempo of the game.

The mindset is simple to say and hard to do: want the ball. Don't hide when an opponent is close. Scan, find a better angle, and trust yourself to solve the pressure. And remember that not every touch has to speed us up. Sometimes you go forward. Sometimes you switch, or carry. And sometimes the best thing you can do is keep the ball while the team reorganizes.

Now a quick run through the four moments.

When we have the ball, scan before you receive whenever there's time, receive side-on, and move again after you pass. Play forward when the advantage is real. When it isn't, recycle the ball or switch it.

When we lose it, counterpress right away if the ball is close and someone is protecting underneath. Otherwise, recover in front of the center backs. And if an outside back has been caught high, delay the counter until she recovers.

When they have the ball, screen the central passing lanes. Step when you're covered, and delay when you're exposed. When they play a long ball, move to where it will land, so you can win the second ball. And when the ball is wide in our third, protect the top of the box and the cutback lane.

When we win it, scan forward first. If an attacker has been released into space, play quickly. If not, secure that first pass and help us organize.

Now think about the teammates around you. You, the other pivot, and the ten form a triangle. One shows for the ball. One protects. One threatens. But it's a rotating triangle, not three fixed dots, and who does each job can change. If one of you drops, another rises. If one advances, another protects underneath. And if the ten moves, the triangle adjusts to her.

With your pivot partner, stay at different heights, keep talking, and never both leave the center. And with your center backs, give them an angle to pass into, protect the space in front of them, and delay counters together.
