# The Position Cards

The next seven tracks are the position cards, and they work differently from the chapters you've heard so far. Each one is a short rehearsal for one role: something to play before a match, so your head is already in the game by the time your feet get there.

Listen to your card about two days before a match, once you know your expected role. If you like, play it again on the way to the game. And if you're somewhere you can, close your eyes. When a track asks you to picture a moment, don't just hear the words. See it: the field, your teammates, where the defender is, the ball arriving. I'll pause after each moment to give you time.

Every card has the same shape. You'll hear your purpose in the role and the mindset to carry into the game. Then come six moments to picture, a quick run through the four moments of the game, and the teammates you work with most.

Near the end, I'll ask what to do when you're unsure. Try to answer before I do. The first time, you probably won't know, and that's fine. It gets easier every time you listen. Then we'll say your cues together, out loud if you can.

This isn't a checklist to memorize. It's a set of pictures to rehearse.

A quick word on numbers. We use the common U.S. numbering, one through eleven, and the right side of the field takes the lower number. So the cards, in order, are: goalkeeper, the one. Outside back, the two and the three. Center back, the four and the five. Pivot, the six and the eight. Winger, the seven and the eleven. Striker, the nine. And attacking midfielder, the ten.

One last thing before you start. These are principles, not restrictions. If you recognize a better solution, take it.
