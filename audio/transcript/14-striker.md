# Striker: Number Nine

You're playing striker today. The nine.

If you're somewhere you can, close your eyes.

Your purpose: occupy the center backs, stretch the defense, connect attacks, and finish chances.

Your mindset: be patient without disappearing.

Even with few touches, you can shape a game through your runs, your pressing, and the center backs you keep busy. If a run isn't working, notice why, and try a different one. Want the next chance. A missed shot should not change the courage of your next one.

Say the next-play mantra with me. [pause] [slows down] Respond. Reflect. Learn. Next play.

Now take a breath. [pause] [slows down] Picture these moments.

You pin a center back, check short as the ten runs beyond you, then get back up high. [long pause]

You drag your defender away, so a winger can drive inside through the gap. [long pause]

You bend a diagonal run across a center back, into the space behind her. [long pause]

The ball is wide, near the end line. You attack the near post, the far post, or the cutback, and then the second ball. [long pause]

You lay the ball off, then move right away into the next space. [long pause]

In transition, a faster winger is breaking through the middle. You support her run instead of racing her. [long pause]

Now, the four moments.

When we have it: make diagonal runs behind, not just straight ones, so defenders can't step up freely. Check to the ball when it helps a combination or opens space behind.

When we lose it: react immediately. Press if the ball is near you and teammates can support. Otherwise, block passes into the middle, and steer play toward our pressing plan.

When they have it: stay connected, and make their buildup predictable. Don't chase every defender. Lead the press with your voice, and with a curved run that blocks a passing lane.

When we win it: decide quickly. Run behind, show to feet, or drag a defender away for a teammate. No advantage? Help us keep the ball.

Your important relationships.

One comes. One goes.

The attacking midfielder, the ten: if you check, she can run beyond you. If she checks, you stay high and pin the center backs. You and the ten can keep cycling during an attack. Positions set the shape; movement changes who fills each space.

The wingers, the seven and the eleven: when you check or move a center back, a winger can run diagonally into that space. Swap lanes when it helps. In transition, whoever has the better lane takes the central run.

When you're unsure, what do you do? See if you can say it before I do. [long pause] Threaten behind first. Then read what your movement creates.

Your cues. Say them with me. [pause] [slows down] Threaten. Move them. Finish.

After the game, ask yourself: Did my movement change what the center backs had to decide? Take that question with you.

These are principles, not restrictions. If you recognize a better solution, take it.
