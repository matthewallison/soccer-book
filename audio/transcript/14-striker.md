# Striker: Number Nine

You're playing striker today. The nine. Let's get your head there before you get to the field.

If you're somewhere you can, close your eyes.

Your job is to occupy their center backs, stretch the defense, connect our attacks, and finish chances.

The mindset for a striker is patience without disappearing. You might not get many touches. That's normal. You can still shape the whole game through your runs, your pressing, and the center backs you keep busy. If a run isn't working, notice why, and try a different one. Want the next chance. And a missed shot should never change the courage of your next one.

Say the next-play mantra with me. [pause] [slows down] Respond. Reflect. Learn. Next play.

Now take a breath. [pause] [slows down] Picture these moments.

You're pinning a center back. As the ten runs beyond you, you check short to the ball, then get back up high. [long pause]

You drag your defender away from the middle, and a winger drives inside through the gap you just opened. [long pause]

You bend a diagonal run across the front of a center back, into the space behind her. [long pause]

The ball is wide, near the end line. You attack the near post, the far post, or the cutback, and then you attack the second ball. [long pause]

You lay the ball off to a teammate, then move right away into the next space. [long pause]

We break in transition, and a faster winger is flying through the middle. You support her run instead of racing her for it. [long pause]

Now a quick run through the four moments.

When we have the ball, make diagonal runs behind, not just straight ones, so their defenders can't step up freely. And check to the ball when it helps a combination, or when it opens space behind you.

When we lose it, react immediately. Press if the ball is near you and teammates can support. Otherwise, block passes into the middle and steer their play toward our pressing plan.

When they have the ball, stay connected to the team, and make their buildup predictable. Don't chase every defender. Lead the press with your voice, and with a curved run that blocks a passing lane.

When we win it, decide quickly: run behind, show to feet, or drag a defender away for a teammate. And if there's no advantage, help us keep the ball.

Now think about the teammates around you. You and the ten work as a pair. One comes. One goes. If you check toward the ball, she can run beyond you. If she checks, you stay high and pin the center backs. And the two of you can keep cycling like that during an attack. Positions set the shape. Movement changes who fills each space.

With the wingers, the seven and the eleven, your movement makes their space. When you check, or pull a center back away, a winger can run diagonally into the gap. Swap lanes with them when it helps. And in transition, whoever has the better lane takes the central run.

So, when you're unsure, what do you do? See if you can say it before I do. [long pause] Threaten behind first. Then read what your movement creates.

Your cues. Say them with me. [pause] [slows down] Threaten. Move them. Finish.

After the game, ask yourself one question: Did my movement change what the center backs had to decide? Take that question with you.

These are principles, not restrictions. If you recognize a better solution, take it.
