# Attacking Midfielder: Number Ten

You're playing attacking midfielder today. The ten. Let's get your head there before you get to the field.

If you're somewhere you can, close your eyes.

Your job is to connect midfield to attack, find useful space between the lines, and help create chances.

The mindset: want the ball in difficult spaces. You are allowed to try the turn, the final pass, the dribble, or the combination. Some of them won't work. That's part of playing this position. Just don't let one failed idea make you cautious.

Now a quick run through the four moments.

When we have the ball, you and the pivots are a rotating triangle. One shows. One protects. One threatens. And who does each job can change. So don't park in one spot. Show between the lines, drop to connect, find a half-space, or run behind. When it's tight, combine. When it opens up, carry, switch, or release a runner.

Remember: small space, combine. Big space, exploit.

When we lose it, react immediately. You're often the closest player to the ball, so counterpress if support is near. Otherwise, recover into midfield and protect central space.

When they have the ball, know the plan for this game: either step up beside the nine, or screen their deepest midfielder. Stay connected to the pivots, and screen passes into the middle. And recognize the pressing triggers, instead of chasing every pass.

When we win it, look forward. Release a runner, or attack the middle before they recover. If that isn't on, help keep the ball while we reorganize.

Now think about the teammates around you. You and the nine work as a pair. One comes. One goes. If she checks, you can run beyond her. If you check, she stays high and pins the center backs. And the two of you can keep cycling like that during an attack. Positions set the shape. Movement changes who fills each space.

With the pivots, the six and the eight, if you drop, one of them can rise. If one of them advances, another protects underneath. And with the wingers, combine in the half-space, and rotate with them without losing our width.

So, when you're unsure, what do you do? See if you can say it before I do. [long pause] Find a different height than the nine, and become available.

After the game, ask yourself one question: Did I find space between the lines, and did I move again after I passed? Take that question with you.

These are principles, not restrictions. If you recognize a better solution, take it.
