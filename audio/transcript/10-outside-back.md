# Outside Back: Numbers Two and Three

You're playing outside back today. The two on the right, or the three on the left.

If you're somewhere you can, close your eyes.

Your purpose: protect the outside channel, give the team a wide outlet, and join the attack without losing defensive balance.

Your mindset: be proactive going forward, and relentless recovering.

Getting forward is not a mistake when it's intelligent and you recover the moment we lose the ball. Read your winger, talk to her, and take the space she isn't using.

Now take a breath. [pause] [slows down] Picture these moments.

A center back is under pressure. You move wide early, open your body, and give her an easy outlet. [long pause]

You receive with open grass ahead. You carry until a defender has to step, then release the winger or the nine. [long pause]

You play a wall pass with your winger. You pass, run past your defender, and get it back. [long pause]

Your winger has the ball. If she cuts inside, you overlap to give width. If she stays wide, you underlap, or support underneath. [long pause]

We lose the ball high up the field. You sprint back on an angle, to where the attacker is going, not behind her. [long pause]

The ball is on the far side. You tuck toward the center backs, spot the far-post runner, and get there first. [long pause]

Now, the four moments.

When we have it: if the line down the touchline is closed, play inside, play backward, or switch. Don't force it. Overlap when it creates something, not out of habit.

When we lose it: react immediately. Counterpress with support if the ball is near and can be won back. Otherwise, sprint back. Cut off the dangerous route to goal first, then curve your run. Don't run to where the attacker is. Run to where she is going.

When they have it: protect inside danger first. See the ball, and the most dangerous opponent or space. Know when to step, delay, or cover. Don't get dragged so wide or so high that the center is exposed. And when the ball is on the far side, stay connected to your center back, ready to expand again when we win it.

When we win it: look for a quick transition if the space is real. Otherwise, be a safe outlet and help us secure possession.

Your important relationships.

Your winger, the seven or the eleven: don't share a lane. If she comes inside, you give width. If she stays wide, you support underneath.

Your nearest center back: stay connected to her line, and talk to her.

The other outside back: don't both go high, unless the pivots stay underneath.

When you're unsure, what do you do? See if you can say it before I do. [long pause] Protect inside first.

Your cues. Say them with me. [pause] [slows down] Angle. Support. Carry. Protect. Recover.

After the game, ask yourself: When we lost it, did I recover on an angle to where the attacker was going, or chase from behind? Take that question with you.

These are principles, not restrictions. If you recognize a better solution, take it.
