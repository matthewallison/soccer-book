# Outside Back: Numbers Two and Three

You're playing outside back today. The two on the right, or the three on the left. Let's get your head there before you get to the field.

If you're somewhere you can, close your eyes.

Your job is to protect the outside channel, give the team a wide outlet, and join the attack without leaving us unbalanced at the back.

The mindset for this role comes in two halves: proactive going forward, and relentless recovering. Getting forward is not a mistake when it's intelligent, and when you recover the moment we lose the ball. So watch your winger. Talk to her. And take the space she isn't using.

Now take a breath. [pause] [slows down] Picture these moments.

Your center back has the ball, and their forward is closing her down. You move wide early, open your body, and give her an easy outlet. [long pause]

You receive with open grass ahead of you. Nobody steps, so you carry, and you keep carrying until a defender has to come out. Then you release the winger, or the nine. [long pause]

You play a wall pass with your winger. You pass, sprint past your defender, and get it back in stride. [long pause]

Your winger has the ball. If she cuts inside, you overlap around the outside to give width. If she stays wide, you underlap inside her, or support underneath. [long pause]

We lose the ball high up the field, and their winger breaks. You sprint back on an angle, toward where she's going, not chasing behind her. [long pause]

The ball is on the far side of the field. You tuck in toward your center backs, spot the runner heading for the far post, and get there first. [long pause]

Now a quick run through the four moments.

When we have the ball and the line down the touchline is closed, don't force it. Play inside, play backward, or switch. And overlap when it creates something, not out of habit.

When we lose it, react immediately. If the ball is near and a teammate is there to support you, counterpress. Otherwise, sprint back. Cut off the dangerous route to goal first, then curve your run.

Remember: don't run to where the attacker is. Run to where she is going.

When they have the ball, protect the inside danger first. Keep the ball in view, along with the most dangerous opponent or space. Know when to step, when to delay, and when to cover. Don't get dragged so wide or so high that the center is left exposed. And when the ball is on the far side, stay connected to your center back, ready to expand again the moment we win it.

When we win it, look for a quick transition if the space is really there. If it isn't, be a safe outlet and help us secure possession.

Now think about the teammates around you. You and your winger, the seven or the eleven, should never share a lane. If she comes inside, you give width. If she stays wide, you support underneath. Your nearest center back needs you connected to her line, and she needs to hear you talking. And with the other outside back, the rule is simple: don't both go high, unless the pivots are staying underneath.

So, when you're unsure, what do you do? See if you can say it before I do. [long pause] Protect inside first.

Your cues. Say them with me. [pause] [slows down] Angle. Support. Carry. Protect. Recover.

After the game, ask yourself one question: When we lost it, did I recover on an angle to where the attacker was going, or chase from behind? Take that question with you.

These are principles, not restrictions. If you recognize a better solution, take it.
