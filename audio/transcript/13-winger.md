# Winger: Numbers Seven and Eleven

You're playing winger today. The seven on the right, or the eleven on the left.

If you're somewhere you can, close your eyes.

Your purpose: stretch the defense, threaten behind, attack favorable one-on-ones, and become dangerous inside when it's on.

Your mindset: be dangerous before you touch the ball.

Your position and your runs should already make defenders uncomfortable. Want the next one-on-one. When a one-on-one favors you, attack it. If a dribble fails, notice why, change your move, and go again.

Say the pressure mantra with me. [pause] [slows down] Want the moment.

Now take a breath. [pause] [slows down] Picture these moments.

You receive wide, with one defender isolated in front of you. You attack her with speed and a change of direction. [long pause]

The nine checks toward the ball. You start wide, and run diagonally into the space she left behind. [long pause]

You escape pressure near the touchline, then carry into open grass until a defender has to step. [long pause]

We win the ball back, and a central lane opens. You become the central runner, even though you started wide. [long pause]

A switch arrives at your feet, with space. Your first touch goes forward, before they can shift. [long pause]

We're attacking down the far side. You arrive at the far post just as the cross comes in. [long pause]

Now, the four moments.

When we have it: start wide, then run diagonally behind, not only straight down the touchline. Make crossing runs that force defenders to decide who follows you. Swap sides with the other winger when it creates an advantage, but keep our width. And use deception before you cross, dribble, or combine.

When we lose it: if you lost it wide, counterpress right away if a teammate is close enough to help. Otherwise, recover into team shape, and track the dangerous outside route.

When they have it: know when to press their outside back, and when to hold shape. Help protect the central passing lanes, and see the ball and the weak-side danger. When the ball is on the far side, tuck inside to keep midfield compact.

When we win it: look immediately for open field to attack. If the transition isn't on, give width and be a clean outlet.

Your important relationships.

Your outside back, the two or the three: don't share a lane. If you go inside, she gives width. If you stay wide, she supports underneath.

The striker, the nine: when she checks, run diagonally behind. Swap lanes with her when it creates an advantage. In transition, whoever has the better lane takes the central run. Sometimes that's you, and the nine supports.

The attacking midfielder, the ten: combine in the half-space, and rotate with her without losing our width.

When you're unsure, what do you do? See if you can say it before I do. [long pause] Create width or threaten depth. Do not disappear.

Your cues. Say them with me. [pause] [slows down] Wide. Diagonal. Threaten. Arrive.

After the game, ask yourself: When the ball was on the far side, was I preparing for the switch, or just watching? Take that question with you.

These are principles, not restrictions. If you recognize a better solution, take it.
