# Center Back: Numbers Four and Five

You're playing center back today. The four or the five. Let's get your head there before you get to the field.

If you're somewhere you can, close your eyes.

Your job is to protect the center of the field, lead the back line, and start our attacks with composure.

For a center back, calm is a weapon. Safe does not automatically mean a clearance. Even near your own goal, even right after a pass has gone wrong, if you have time and space, solve the soccer problem. And use your voice early. You're the one telling the line when to step, when to drop, and when to cover.

Say the game mantra with me. [pause] [slows down] Calm mind. Fast soccer.

Now take a breath. [pause] [slows down] Picture these moments.

Your goalkeeper rolls the ball out to you. You check your shoulder, open your body, and take your first touch forward. [long pause]

Nobody is pressing you. So you carry, until one of their players steps out to stop you. Then you find the midfielder she just left free. [long pause]

The side with the ball is getting crowded, and their players are shifting over. You switch it to the open side. [long pause]

Their press is pushing high, and you see our far-side winger free. You hit a purposeful diagonal into her path. [long pause]

They counter, and an attacker is running straight at you. You stay on your feet, slow her down, and wait for help to arrive. [long pause]

Your partner steps up to win the ball. You tuck in behind her and cover the space she just left. [long pause]

Now a quick run through the four moments.

When we have the ball, your center back partner is an option, not an automatic pass. When the lane forward is closed, carry sideways to change the angle. When the lane into midfield is really there, find a midfielder's feet, and after you pass, step up to support.

When we lose it, protect the central route to goal first, and then decide whether to step, delay, or recover. If an outside back has been caught high, delay the counter until she gets back. And when you recover, run on an angle toward goal, rather than chasing.

When they have the ball, read the pressure on it. If the ball is pressured, hold the line or push up together. If it isn't, drop a few steps together. Moving as one line is how you manage offside. On crosses, stay goal-side, see both the ball and the runner, and attack the ball in front of you. And read passes early, so you can intercept when you know you can win it.

When we win it, scan forward. If there's a real transition advantage, use it. If not, secure the ball and start possession calmly.

Now think about the teammates around you. You and your center back partner never attack the same problem together. Don't both go to the same attacker, and don't both leave her free.

Remember: one steps, one covers.

The pivots, the six and the eight, give you an angle to pass into and protect the space in front of you, and when the other team counters, you delay it together. Your goalkeeper is your outlet and your eyes behind you. Keep her available, and listen to her calls. And your outside backs are your early wide outlets. Use them, and keep them connected to your line.

So, when you're unsure, what do you do? See if you can say it before I do. [long pause] Protect the center. Stay balanced. Buy the team time.

Your cues. Say them with me. [pause] [slows down] Calm. Talk. Carry. Connect. Delay.

After the game, ask yourself one question: When nobody pressured me, did I carry or find a midfielder's feet, or did I clear it? Take that question with you.

These are principles, not restrictions. If you recognize a better solution, take it.
