# Goalkeeper: Number One

You're playing goalkeeper today. The number one. Let's get your head there before you get to the field.

If you're somewhere you can, close your eyes.

Your job has three parts. Protect the goal. Organize the team in front of you. And be our eleventh possession player, because a goalkeeper who's comfortable with the ball gives the whole team an extra option.

Bring a calm, decisive mind into this game. If you decide to come, come. If you decide to play, play. The costly goalkeeping moments are usually half-decisions. You don't need to save everything, and you don't need to pass perfectly. You just need to make the next decision completely.

Use your voice early, before danger develops. You can see the whole field in front of you, and your teammates can't, so your voice leads the team. And after a mistake, or after a goal, don't stand there replaying it. Get set, and organize right away.

Say the next-play mantra with me. [pause] [slows down] Respond. Reflect. Learn. Next play.

Now take a breath. [pause] [slows down] Picture these moments.

A back pass is rolling toward you, and their forward is chasing it. You open your body, touch it across, and play it to the opposite side, away from her. [long pause]

Your center back has the ball near the touchline, and she's under pressure. You move to an angle behind her and call early for the pass back, so she knows it's on. [long pause]

You catch a shot cleanly. Before their attackers have turned around, you scan, spot an open teammate, and throw or roll it to her while the other team is still resetting. [long pause]

A through ball slides in behind your back line. You decide to come, and you come all the way, sweeping it up before their striker gets there. [long pause]

A cross is swinging in from the wing. You decide early, call it loudly, and claim it. Or, if it's crowded, you punch it clear. [long pause]

They shoot. You get set, and whatever happens, save or goal, you get straight back up and organize the team. [long pause]

Now a quick run through the four moments.

When we have the ball, treat back passes as normal soccer, not emergencies, and use both feet. Before a goal kick, read their press. If they leave space, play short. If they commit everyone forward, go beyond them. Distribute with intention. Punt or go long when it's truly the better option, not out of habit.

When we lose the ball, reorganize quickly behind it, and talk. Tell your team about depth, runners, and danger. And when our line is high, start higher yourself, so you can sweep the space behind it.

When they have the ball, manage your depth compared to your back line, and point out the runners your defenders can't see.

And when we win it, scan right away. Can we restart possession quickly and intelligently? If the ball is in your hands, release it within eight seconds, or they get a corner kick.

Remember: quick possession before hopeful distance.

Now think about the teammates you work with most. Your center backs, the four and the five, need you as their outlet and their extra eyes. Tell them early whether to step, drop, or play it back to you. And your outside backs and pivots are often the quickest way out. A fast throw or pass to whichever one is free can beat the press before it's set.

So, when you're unsure, what do you do? See if you can say it before I do. [long pause] Get set. Communicate. Make the next decision fully.

Your cues. Say them with me. [pause] [slows down] Organize. Decide. Commit. Distribute.

After the game, ask yourself one question: After I collected the ball, did I scan and restart possession quickly, or punt out of habit? Take that question with you.

These are principles, not restrictions. If you recognize a better solution, take it.
