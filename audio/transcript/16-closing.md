Imagine someone who has never seen us play walks up to the field and watches for five minutes. They can't hear the coach. What would they notice?

They'd see players turning their heads before the ball arrives, and hear teammates giving each other useful information. They'd see passes with a purpose, and a team that trains at speed but stays calm when the game gets tense. The moment the ball changes hands, they'd see players react instantly. They'd see players step into pressure moments instead of hiding from them, pick each other up after mistakes, and take ownership of how they're improving.

With the ball, they'd see players ask for it and support it. Players who disguise what they're about to do, move again after passing, carry into open space, switch the field, use the weak side, and find the third player. Players who can tell when the game is small and when it's big.

And without the ball, they'd see a team defending together: delaying instead of diving in, and recovering on good angles instead of chasing.
