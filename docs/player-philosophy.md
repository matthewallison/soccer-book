# High-Level Player Sheet

This is the short version. These are the ideas every player should know by memory.

## Our three team mantras

### Practice
**SCAN. TALK. INTENTION. FASTER.**

- **Scan:** collect information before you need it.
- **Talk:** give teammates useful information they cannot see.
- **Intention:** every action should have a reason.
- **Faster:** push the speed at which the first three can still be done correctly.

### Games
**CALM MIND. FAST SOCCER.**

Fast soccer does not mean panic. It means seeing the game early, deciding quickly, and executing with control.

### After mistakes
**RESPOND. REFLECT. LEARN. NEXT PLAY.**

Creative play and risk-taking will create mistakes. Mistakes should be learning moments, never reasons for shame or embarrassment.

## How we want to play

- **No hiding. No watching. Influence the game.**
- Move after you pass.
- Everyone is a potential outlet. If you do not become an option, you are making the ball carrier's problem harder.
- Progress when an advantage exists. Keep the ball when it does not.
- Carry open space until a defender has to react.
- Look diagonal, sideways, backward, and forward.
- Show one thing and do another. **Look like you're changing your mind.**
- Use width, depth, different heights, and good spacing.
- Strong side attracts. Weak side prepares.
- On loss: counterpress if we can win it; otherwise recover immediately.
- Protect the middle and delay when necessary.
- Defend from positions that can also help us attack when we win the ball.
- Head with purpose, not habit.

## Brave soccer

We want players to turn, carry, dribble, disguise passes, combine, and try creative solutions when they recognize the opportunity.

A failed action can still come from a good decision.

**We do not punish good ideas simply because the execution failed.**

After a mistake, ask:

1. What did I see?
2. Was the idea reasonable?
3. Was the decision good?
4. Did the execution fail?
5. What can I learn for next time?

Then move on.

## Compete to win, develop for the long term

**Winning is not the primary measure of development, but competing to win is part of development.**

We want every player to care about winning, compete hard, recover, defend, communicate, and support teammates. But we will not sacrifice long-term growth simply to improve today's scoreline.

## Team development culture

We are responsible not only for our own development, but for helping create an environment where teammates improve too.

**Your effort is part of your teammate's training.**

If you defend casually, your teammate does not learn to beat real pressure. If you jog through transition, teammates do not learn to solve realistic transition problems.

We compete with each other so we can become better together.

### Peer support

**Help each other learn. Don't grade each other.**

- Describe, don't judge.
- Ask before giving advice.
- Use shared team language.
- Praise useful behavior specifically.
- Support teammates after mistakes.
- Leave selection, discipline, and major corrections to the coach.

## Development ownership

Every player should know:

- one current strength to build on;
- one current development priority;
- what progress would look like.

These should change as the player improves.

We want players who eventually understand enough soccer to coach themselves.
