# High-Level Player Sheet

This is the short version. Know the four mantras by memory. Learn the bold principles over time, and use this sheet as a quick reference. The fuller explanations live in [Team Culture, Courage, Leadership & Self-Coaching](team-culture-and-leadership.md) and, for how we play, [Communication & Movement Fundamentals](communication-and-movement.md).

## Our four team mantras

We say our mantras out loud, together: the practice mantra is the first thing we say at every practice; the game mantra is the first thing we say in every game warm-up.

### Practice
**SCAN. TALK. INTENTION. FASTER.**

- **Scan:** collect information before you need it.
- **Talk:** give teammates useful information they cannot see.
- **Intention:** every action should have a reason.
- **Faster:** push the speed at which the first three can still be done correctly.

### Games
**CALM MIND. FAST SOCCER.**

Fast soccer does not mean panic. It means seeing early, deciding quickly, and executing with control. Playing quickly is not getting rid of the ball quickly: it can mean holding it an extra second because you already scanned, playing backward, or carrying 15 yards because nobody stepped.

### After mistakes
**RESPOND. REFLECT. LEARN. NEXT PLAY.**

Creative play and risk-taking will create mistakes. Mistakes should be learning moments, never reasons for shame or embarrassment.

### Pressure moments
**WANT THE MOMENT.**

Nerves are normal. They are your body getting ready. Step forward anyway.

## How we want to play

**No hiding. No watching. Influence the game.**

At every moment, ask: **Where could I go right now that would make me useful?**

### When we have it

- Everyone is a potential outlet. Not being an option makes the ball carrier's problem harder.
- Move on the pass. Move again after you pass.
- Use width, depth, different heights, and good spacing. Strong side attracts. Weak side prepares.
- Look diagonal, sideways, backward, and forward.
- Progress when we have an advantage. Keep the ball when we don't.
- Show one thing and do another. Look like you're changing your mind.

### When the field opens

Rondos teach us how to survive when the game gets small. Soccer also requires us to recognize when the game becomes big.

- **Small space: combine. Big space: exploit.**
- When we escape pressure, recognize that the problem has changed. Look up.
- Do not keep playing five-yard passes in the same crowded area after the field opens.
- Carry open grass, switch the field, find a runner, or attack before the defense recovers.
- A longer pass with a clear purpose is not a clearance.
- **Find the biggest useful space.**

**SMALL SPACE: COMBINE. BIG SPACE: EXPLOIT.**

The goal is not to pass short or to pass long. The goal is to recognize the space and use the right solution.

### Without the ball and in transition

- Protect dangerous space first. Get tighter as the ball and danger get closer.
- Protect the middle and delay when necessary.
- On loss: counterpress if we can win it; otherwise recover into shape immediately.
- Defend from a position you can attack from.
- Head with purpose, not habit.

## Brave soccer

We want players to turn, carry, dribble, disguise passes, **play through pressure**, combine, and try creative solutions when they recognize the opportunity.

**We will correct careless decisions, but we will not punish intelligent ideas simply because they did not work.**

Careless means ignoring the picture in front of you or what we have worked on in training.

**In the game: respond, then play the next action.** Later (at a break, after the game, or on film), reflect with the five questions in [Team Culture: Mistakes are learning moments](team-culture-and-leadership.md#mistakes-are-learning-moments).

## Want the moment

The goal is not to eliminate fear. The goal is to stop fear from making the decision. At U14, the real loss is not missing the penalty. The real loss is avoiding the chance to learn how you respond under pressure.

**We should fear wasting developmental opportunities more than we fear temporary failure.**

You may be nervous. You may miss. Neither is shameful. Wanting the moment never means playing through pain or hiding an injury. Tell a parent and your coach what hurts; see [Injuries, Recovery, and Return to Play](injuries-and-return-to-play.md). For the pressure routine and penalty kicks, see [Team Culture: Want the moment](team-culture-and-leadership.md#want-the-moment).

## Compete to win, develop for the long term

**Winning is not the primary measure of development, but competing to win is part of development.**

That means: **Want to win. Play for the next action, not the scoreboard.**

## Everyone leads

Leadership belongs to every player, not only captains. Not every leader is loud. See [Team Culture: Everyone leads](team-culture-and-leadership.md#everyone-leads).

**See what the team needs, and help provide it.**

## Coach yourself

**Be coachable — and learn to coach yourself.**

In games, self-coaching is a loop: scan → understand → decide → execute → reflect → adjust. See [Team Culture: Every player is also her own coach](team-culture-and-leadership.md#every-player-is-also-her-own-coach).

After every practice, ask: **What soccer problem did today's practice teach me to solve?**

## Team development culture

**Your effort is part of your teammate's training.**

We compete hard against each other **to make each other better, not to rank each other.** See [Team Culture: Your effort is part of your teammate's training](team-culture-and-leadership.md#your-effort-is-part-of-your-teammates-training).

### Peer support

**Help each other learn. Don't grade each other.**

See [Team Culture: Help each other learn](team-culture-and-leadership.md#help-each-other-learn).

- Describe, don't judge. Ask before giving advice.
- Use shared team language. Praise useful behavior specifically.
- Support teammates after mistakes. Body language counts: no sighs or eye-rolls.
- In games, give information and encouragement. Save advice for later.
- Leave selection, discipline, and major corrections to the coach.

## Development ownership

Know one current strength to build on, one current development priority chosen with the coach, and what progress would look like. These change as you improve. **Your team role is where you help the team today. Your development role is the complete player you are becoming.**

Want a different role or more minutes? Ask the coach yourself: **"What would I need to show to earn that?"** Talk with the coach during office hours at the field, or send a film question through the team app, with a parent included. See [Team Culture: Asking for opportunities](team-culture-and-leadership.md#asking-for-opportunities).
