# High-Level Player Sheet

This is the short version. These are the ideas every player should know by memory. The fuller explanations live in [Team Culture, Courage, Leadership & Self-Coaching](team-culture-and-leadership.md).

## Our four team mantras

We say our mantras out loud, together: the practice mantra is the first thing we say at every practice; the game mantra is the first thing we say in every game warm-up.

### Practice
**SCAN. TALK. INTENTION. FASTER.**

- **Scan:** collect information before you need it.
- **Talk:** give teammates useful information they cannot see.
- **Intention:** every action should have a reason.
- **Faster:** push the speed at which the first three can still be done correctly.

### Games
**CALM MIND. FAST SOCCER.**

Fast soccer does not mean panic. It means seeing the game early, deciding quickly, and executing with control. Playing quickly is not getting rid of the ball quickly. Fast soccer can mean holding it an extra second because you already scanned, playing backward, or carrying 15 yards because nobody stepped.

### After mistakes
**RESPOND. REFLECT. LEARN. NEXT PLAY.**

Creative play and risk-taking will create mistakes. Mistakes should be learning moments, never reasons for shame or embarrassment.

### Pressure moments
**WANT THE MOMENT.**

Nerves are normal. They are your body getting ready. Step forward anyway.

## How we want to play

**No hiding. No watching. Influence the game.**

At every moment, ask: **Where could I go right now that would make me useful?**

### When we have it

- Everyone is a potential outlet. Not being an option makes the ball carrier's problem harder.
- Move on the pass. Move again after you pass.
- Use width, depth, different heights, and good spacing. Strong side attracts. Weak side prepares.
- Look diagonal, sideways, backward, and forward.
- Progress when an advantage exists. Keep the ball when it does not.
- Show one thing and do another. Look like you're changing your mind.

### When the field opens

- **Small space: combine. Big space: exploit.**
- When we escape pressure, recognize that the problem has changed.
- Do not stay trapped in the small-space game after the field opens.
- Carry open grass until a defender has to react, switch the field, find the weak side, or attack before the defense recovers.
- A longer pass with a clear purpose is not a clearance.
- **Find the biggest useful space.**

### Without the ball and in transition

- Protect dangerous space first. Get tighter as the ball and danger get closer.
- Protect the middle and delay when necessary.
- On loss: counterpress if we can win it; otherwise recover into shape immediately.
- Defend from positions that can also help us attack when we win the ball.
- Head with purpose, not habit.

## Use the whole field

Rondos teach us how to survive when the game gets small. Soccer also requires us to recognize when the game becomes big. When we escape pressure, do not automatically keep playing five-yard passes in the same crowded area. Look up. If space has opened:

- carry it
- switch it
- attack it
- find a runner
- or play a longer pass with purpose

**SMALL SPACE: COMBINE. BIG SPACE: EXPLOIT.**

The goal is not to pass short. The goal is not to pass long. The goal is to recognize the space and use the right solution.

## Brave soccer

We want players to turn, carry, dribble, disguise passes, **play through pressure**, combine, and try creative solutions when they recognize the opportunity.

**We will correct careless decisions, but we will not punish intelligent ideas simply because they did not work.**

Careless means ignoring the picture in front of you or what we have worked on in training. At this age, the real loss is often not the failed action. It is avoiding a valuable development moment because of fear.

**In the game: respond, then play the next action.**

Later (at a break, after the game, or on film), ask:

1. What did I see?
2. What information did I miss?
3. Was the decision reasonable?
4. Did the execution fail?
5. What can I try next time?

## Want the moment

The goal is not to eliminate fear. The goal is to stop fear from making the decision.

**We should fear wasting developmental opportunities more than we fear temporary failure.**

You may be nervous. You may miss. Neither is shameful. For the pressure routine and penalty kicks, see [Want the moment](team-culture-and-leadership.md#want-the-moment).

## Compete to win, develop for the long term

**Winning is not the primary measure of development, but competing to win is part of development.**

**Want to win. Play for the next action, not the scoreboard.**

## Everyone leads

Captains may have a formal role, but leadership belongs to every player. Not every leader is loud. We want a team of leaders, not one or two leaders surrounded by followers. See [Everyone leads](team-culture-and-leadership.md#everyone-leads).

**See what the team needs, and help provide it.**

## Coach yourself

**Be coachable — and learn to coach yourself.**

Become a student of the game. In games, self-coaching is a loop: scan → understand → decide → execute → reflect → adjust. See [Every player is also her own coach](team-culture-and-leadership.md#every-player-is-also-her-own-coach).

After every practice, ask: **What soccer problem did today's practice teach me to solve?**

## Team development culture

**Your effort is part of your teammate's training.**

**We compete hard against each other to make each other better, not to rank each other.**

### Peer support

**Help each other learn. Don't grade each other.**

- Describe, don't judge. Ask before giving advice.
- Use shared team language. Praise useful behavior specifically.
- Support teammates after mistakes. Body language counts: no sighs or eye-rolls.
- In games, give information and encouragement. Save advice for later.
- Leave selection, discipline, and major corrections to the coach.

## Development ownership

Know one current strength to build on, one current development priority chosen with the coach, and what progress would look like. These change as you improve.

**Your team role is where you help the team today. Your development role is the complete player you are becoming.**

Want a different role or more minutes? Ask the coach yourself: **"What would I need to show to earn that?"** Talk with the coach during after-practice time at the field, or send a film question by message with a parent included. See [Asking for opportunities](team-culture-and-leadership.md#asking-for-opportunities).
