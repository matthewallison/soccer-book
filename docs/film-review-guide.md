# Film Review Guide

The purpose of film study is not to decide whether you "played well." It is to learn how to see the game more clearly and become your own coach.

## The most important rule

**Pause before you know the outcome.**

If you wait until after the play ends, it is too easy to judge the decision only by what happened.

A good decision can fail because of execution.
A poor decision can succeed because of luck.

## Review sequence

For each useful clip:

1. **Pause before the action.**
2. **Scan the picture.** What information should the player know?
3. **List the realistic options.** Try to find more than one.
4. **Play each option forward 2–4 actions.** What might happen next?
5. **Find one non-obvious option.** Could deception or creativity change the picture?
6. **Watch the play.**
7. **Judge decision and execution separately.**
8. **Write down one useful lesson.**

## Questions to ask

### Before receiving

- Did I scan early enough?
- Where was pressure coming from?
- Where was my best support?
- Was there open space I could have carried into?
- What did my body shape allow me to see?

### In possession

- Did I recognize the best advantage?
- Did I pass simply because someone was close?
- Could I have carried farther?
- Was a switch available?
- Could I have fixed a defender first?
- Did my pass help the receiver's next action?
- Could deception have created a better option?

### After passing

- Did I move again?
- Did I become another outlet?
- Did I create space for someone else?
- Did I prepare for a possible turnover?

### Defending

- Did I protect the most dangerous space?
- Could I see the ball and the dangerous opponent/space?
- Did I step, delay, cover, or recover appropriately?
- If recovering, did I use a good pursuit angle or simply chase from behind?

### Transition

After losing it:

- Could we counterpress?
- If not, did I recover immediately?
- Was I already in a useful rest-defense position?

After winning it:

- Did I scan forward first?
- Was there a real breakaway opportunity?
- If not, did we secure possession?

## Watch away from the ball

Some of the most important learning happens where the ball is not.

Pick a player and watch only her for 5–10 minutes.

Notice:

- how often she scans;
- when she moves before the pass;
- how she creates angles;
- when she stays wide or moves inside;
- how she reacts after passing;
- what she does on the weak side;
- how she prepares for transitions;
- how much she influences play without touching the ball.

## Review one theme at a time

Do not try to analyze an entire game at once.

Choose 6–10 useful clips around one theme, such as:

- receiving under pressure;
- off-ball movement;
- carrying into space;
- switching play;
- defensive positioning;
- pursuit angles;
- transition reaction;
- deception;
- weak-side behavior.

## Three-part self-review

After a game, identify:

### One thing I did well
Something to continue and strengthen.

### One thing I need to improve
A specific behavior, not a vague judgment.

### One solution I want to try
A concrete action for the next practice or game.

## Watch the next developmental level

Professional matches are useful, but players should also watch soccer only 2–3 years above their current level.

Search for full-match video using phrases like:

- `U16 ECNL girls full match`
- `U16 ECNL National full game`
- `2010 ECNL girls full match`
- `ECNL U16 national playoffs`
- `U16 girls academy full match`
- specific strong clubs plus `U16 full match`

Prefer **full matches**, not highlights.

Highlights teach moments. Full matches teach soccer.

## What to compare across levels

Compare your own game, a strong U16 game, and a professional women's game using the same questions:

- How quickly do players reposition after passing?
- How often do they scan before receiving?
- How fast does the ball move without becoming rushed?
- How often do they carry open space?
- How often do they switch fields intentionally?
- What do weak-side players do?
- What happens immediately after possession changes?
- How often are defenders forced to make decisions by movement or deception?

## Position study

When preparing for a role, watch one strong player in that position and ignore the ball whenever she does not have it.

Ask:

- Where does she stand when the ball is on the opposite side?
- How does she create an outlet?
- What does she do after passing?
- When does she step, drop, press, or delay?
- What does she do immediately when possession changes?

## Final principle

Film review is not about proving that a player was right or wrong.

It is about building a better mental picture of the game.

**See more. Understand more. Choose better.**
