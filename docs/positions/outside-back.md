# Outside Back (#2 / #3) — Position Card

## My purpose

Protect the outside channel, give the team a wide outlet, and join the attack without losing defensive balance.

## Pregame mindset

**Be proactive going forward and relentless recovering.**

Getting forward is not a mistake when it is intelligent and you recover the moment we lose the ball. Read your winger, talk to her, and take the space she is not using.

## Picture these moments

- Picture a center back under pressure: you move wide early, open your body, and give her an easy outlet.
- Picture receiving with open grass ahead: you carry until a defender has to step, then release the winger or the 9.
- Picture a wall pass with your winger: you pass, run past your defender, and get it back.
- Picture your winger on the ball: if she cuts inside, you overlap to give width; if she stays wide, you underlap or support underneath.
- Picture losing the ball high: you sprint back on an angle to where the attacker is going, not behind her.
- Picture the ball on the far side: you tuck toward the center backs, spot the far-post runner, and get there first.

## When we have it

- Play inside, backward, or switch when down the line is closed — don't force it.
- Overlap when it creates something, not by habit.

## When we lose it

- React immediately: counterpress with support if the ball is near and recoverable.
- Otherwise sprint back: cut off the dangerous route to goal first, then curve the run.

**Do not run to where the attacker is. Run to where she is going.**

## When they have it

- Protect inside danger first: see the ball and the most dangerous opponent or space.
- Know when to step, delay, or cover.
- Don't get dragged so wide or high that the center becomes exposed.
- Far side: stay connected to your center back; expand again when we win it.

## When we win it

- Look for a quick transition if the space is real.
- Otherwise become a safe outlet and help us secure possession.

## Important relationships

- Your winger (#7 / #11): don't share a lane. If she comes inside, give width. If she stays wide, support underneath.
- Nearest center back (#4 / #5): stay connected to her line, and talk to her.
- The other outside back (#2 / #3): don't both go high unless the pivots (#6 / #8) stay underneath.

## When you're unsure

**Protect inside first.**

## My cues

**Angle. Support. Carry. Protect. Recover.**

**These are principles, not restrictions. If you recognize a better solution, take it.**
