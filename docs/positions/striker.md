# Striker (#9) — Position Card

## My purpose

Occupy the center backs, stretch the defense, connect attacks, and finish chances.

## Pregame mindset

**Be patient without disappearing.**

Few touches can still shape a game through your runs, your pressing, and the center backs you keep busy. If a run isn't working, notice why and try a different one. **Want the next chance.** A missed shot should not change the courage of your next one: **RESPOND. REFLECT. LEARN. NEXT PLAY.**

## Picture these moments

- Picture pinning a center back, checking short as the 10 runs beyond you, then getting back up high.
- Picture dragging your defender away so a winger can drive inside through the gap.
- Picture bending a diagonal run across a center back into the space behind her.
- Picture the ball wide near the end line: you attack near post, far post, or the cutback, then the second ball.
- Picture laying the ball off, then moving right away into the next space.
- Picture a faster winger breaking centrally in transition: you support her run instead of racing her.

## When we have it

- Make diagonal runs behind, not just straight ones, so defenders can't step up freely.
- Check to the ball when it helps a combination or opens space behind.

## When we lose it

- React immediately: press if the ball is near you and teammates can support.
- Otherwise block passes into the middle and steer play toward our pressing plan.

## When they have it

- Stay connected and make their buildup predictable; don't chase every defender.
- Lead the press with your voice and a curved run that blocks a passing lane.

## When we win it

- Decide quickly: run behind, show to feet, or drag a defender away for a teammate.
- No advantage? Help us keep the ball.

## Important relationships

**One comes. One goes.**

- Attacking midfielder (#10): if you check, she can run beyond you. If she checks, you stay high and pin the center backs.
- You and the 10 can keep cycling during an attack. Positions set the shape; movement changes who fills each space.
- Wingers (#7 / #11): when you check or move a center back, a winger can run diagonally into that space.
- Swap lanes when it helps. In transition, whoever has the better lane takes the central run.

## When you're unsure

**Threaten behind first. Then read what your movement creates.**

## My cues

**Threaten. Move them. Finish.**

**These are principles, not restrictions. If you recognize a better solution, take it.**
