# Parent Partnership

Parents are partners in a player-first development environment. Players do the playing and, more and more, their own thinking and talking. Coaches teach, plan, and set roles. Families make the rest possible: rides, meals, sleep, and the steady message that she is loved whatever the score. To judge the coaching itself, see [What Good Coaching Looks Like](good-coaching-signs.md).

## Player first

**Player first. Parent second. Safety immediately.**

- Questions about role, minutes, or development start with the player. She books office hours, and you help her prepare (see [Asking for opportunities](team-culture-and-leadership.md#asking-for-opportunities)).
- If you still have a concern about a match, wait 24 hours, then ask the coach for a conversation.
- Any safety, health, or welfare concern is raised right away, with no waiting period.

## On the sideline

- Cheer for the whole team, especially effort and brave ideas.
- Leave instruction to the coaches. Two voices giving different directions confuse players.
- Leave officials alone.
- Never criticize a missed penalty, or a player who decides not to take one. After any mistake, cheer the next play.

**Talk about what bodies can do, never how they look.**

Praise strong, fast, balanced, and brave. No comments about weight, shape, or appearance from coaches, parents, or teammates.

## After the game

- **"I love watching you play."**
- Let her start the soccer conversation: "Want to talk about it now or later?"
- Save critique for later. Keep it honest and short, and use the team's words: What did you see? Next play.
- No player carries a result alone. Don't replay one moment as the reason the game was lost.

## Helping at home

Extra work at home is optional. Use the team's words (scan, carry, next play), offer rather than require, and leave new tactical instruction to the coach. The [Home Development Menu](home-development.md) has short sessions she can choose from. Count the whole week, and protect sleep: teens need 8–10 hours. See [Recovery basics](home-development.md#recovery-basics).

## Health signals

**Fuel and periods.** Growing athletes need enough total food, including iron, calcium, and vitamin D. Eating too little for the training load (low energy availability) harms both health and performance. Once periods have started, going about 3 months or more without one is not a normal part of hard training. The same goes for no period by age 15. See a doctor.

**Head injuries.** After any blow to the head or body, a player who seems dazed, unsteady, or "not right" comes out right away. Unless a health care professional at the field evaluates and clears her, she is done for the day.

**When in doubt, sit her out.**

Returning after a concussion is a medical process, not a coaching or family decision: step by step with her health care provider, and cleared (in writing where your state requires it) before contact, heading, or full training. Tell the coach about any concussion, even one away from soccer.

## Communication and safeguarding

**Club safeguarding policy comes first.** Every adult working with the team, parent volunteers included, keeps SafeSport training and background screening current. Where this handbook differs, club policy wins. Check it each season.

**Open. Observable. About soccer.**

- Team news and film questions go through one club-approved team app. Parent accounts are the main contact, and players join only if the app's age rules allow.
- Every coach message to a player includes her parent or another team adult, even when the player writes first. Messages stay about soccer, with no social-media direct messages or disappearing-message apps.
- Office hours: the coach stays 15 minutes after every practice, and a player can book up to 5 minutes, in person or through the team app with a parent included.
- Talks happen at the field, in view of others. **Out of earshot is fine. Out of sight is not.** Parents are welcome to listen in, and no player waits alone with one coach for her ride.
- Carpools follow club policy. No player rides alone with one unrelated adult without her parent's advance written permission, and drivers pick up their own child first and drop her off last when possible.
- Parents are encouraged to take the U.S. Center for SafeSport's free parent training each year.

Report safety or abuse concerns immediately, never at office hours: 911 in an emergency, local authorities for suspected child abuse, the U.S. Center for SafeSport (833-587-7233 or online).

## Film

**Team film stays with the team.** Watch game film through the team's private link or app, and don't repost it publicly. If you want a clip of your daughter taken down, ask, and it comes down. See [Sharing film safely](film-review-guide.md#sharing-film-safely).
