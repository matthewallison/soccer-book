# Communication & Movement Fundamentals

Shared language makes the team faster because players do not have to guess what teammates mean.

The goal is not constant shouting. Communication should be early, specific, useful, and positive.

## Communication glossary

### Information cues

These give the player information rather than making the decision for her.

- **Time** — you have room to control and look up.
- **Man on** — pressure is arriving immediately.
- **Turn** — you can receive and face forward.
- **Left shoulder / right shoulder** — check that side now.
- **Inside / outside** — information about pressure, support, or space.

### Action cues

These suggest a useful action, but the player on the ball still owns the final decision.

- **Carry** — attack the open space with the ball.
- **Switch** — move play toward the opposite side.
- **Set** — bounce or lay the ball back quickly.
- **Hold** — keep possession; do not force progression.
- **Drop** — recover or offer a deeper support option.
- **Step** — close or intercept now.
- **Delay** — buy time; do not dive in.
- **Cover** — I am supporting behind/inside you.
- **Leave** — let the ball run to another player.
- **Line** — option down the touchline.
- **Inside** — inside support is available.

Over time, prefer useful information over remote-control commands.

## Receiving posture

### Side-on when possible

Open the hips enough to see both where the ball came from and where the next action may go.

Do not force an exaggerated side-on position if pressure or the pass angle makes another posture safer.

### Meet the ball

Do not wait flat-footed for a pass that a defender can attack.

Step toward the pass when appropriate and arrive before the defender.

**Meet the ball** applies to:

- checking to feet;
- midfield receiving;
- defenders intercepting;
- attacking crosses and loose balls.

### First touch begins the next action

A good first touch is not simply stopping the ball.

Use the arriving ball to:

- turn;
- escape pressure;
- carry;
- protect;
- set up the next pass.

## Athletic posture

General principles:

- knees soft;
- weight balanced;
- feet alive;
- avoid standing tall and locked;
- be ready to move in more than one direction.

## 1v1 defensive posture

### Approach fast, arrive controlled

Close space quickly, then shorten the final steps so the attacker cannot beat you with one touch.

### Low and mobile

Lower the center of mass enough to move laterally, but do not sink so low that movement becomes slow.

### Avoid unnecessary crossing of the feet

Use controlled lateral and crossover movement according to the distance and speed of the attacker.

### Show rather than dive

Use body angle to influence the attacker toward less dangerous space.

## Pursuit angles

A recovering defender should not simply chase the attacker's back.

**Do not run to where the attacker is. Run to where she is going.**

Aim first toward the dangerous lane between the attacker and goal, then curve the recovery run as the gap closes.

This helps the defender:

- cut off central space;
- shorten the route to goal;
- force the attacker wider;
- recover without needing to win a pure footrace from behind.

## Movement timing

### Move on the pass, not after it

Players should anticipate the next picture while the ball is traveling.

### Arrive, don't camp

Being in a useful space too early can allow defenders to settle and block the lane.

Move into the space as the passing lane develops.

### Different heights

Teammates should avoid standing in flat horizontal lines when possible.

Staggering creates angles and third-player options.

### Pass and move

After every pass, ask:

**Where can I help next?**

## Defensive vision

Players should gather information about:

- ball;
- goal;
- teammates;
- opponents;
- dangerous space.

Useful cue:

**See the ball and the most dangerous information around you.**

Do not become so focused on a single mark that you lose the larger picture.

## Transition posture

Good defensive positioning should help the next attack.

Good attacking positioning should help the next defensive transition.

**Defend from a position you can attack from.**

## Final standard

Good movement should make teammates' decisions easier.

**Create an angle. Meet the ball. Stay balanced. Move again.**
