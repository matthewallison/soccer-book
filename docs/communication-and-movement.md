# Communication & Movement Fundamentals

Shared language makes the team faster because nobody has to guess what a teammate means.

The goal is not constant shouting. Communication should be early, specific, useful, and positive.

## Communication glossary

### Information cues

These give the player information rather than making the decision for her.

- **Time** — you have room to control and look up.
- **Man on** — pressure is arriving immediately.
- **Turn** — you can receive and face forward.
- **Left shoulder / right shoulder** — check that side now.
- **Inside / outside** — where the pressure, support, or space is (for example, support is available inside).
- **Cover** — I am behind or inside you as support.
- **Line** — option down the touchline.

### Action cues

These suggest a useful action, but the player who hears the cue still owns the final decision.

- **Set** — bounce or lay the ball back quickly.
- **Hold** — keep possession; do not force the ball forward.
- **Drop** — recover or offer a deeper support option.
- **Step** — close or intercept now.
- **Delay** — buy time; do not dive in.
- **Leave** — let the ball run to another player.

### Big-space cues

These tell the team that the field has opened. Anyone can call them, but the player on the ball still owns the final decision.

- **Expand** — we have escaped pressure; use the larger space now.
- **Switch** — change the point of attack to the weak side (the side away from the ball).
- **Carry** — open grass is available; attack it with the ball.
- **Go** — an immediate transition or breakaway opportunity exists.

Over time, prefer useful information over remote-control commands.

## Changing the size of the game

Recognize whether the soccer problem in front of you is a small-space problem or a large-space problem.

For a small-space problem, use:

- quick support
- short combinations
- third-player actions (A → B → C)
- wall passes
- tight receiving
- deception

For a large-space problem, use:

- carrying
- longer passing
- switches
- running beyond
- weak-side movement
- acceleration into open grass

When we escape pressure, the problem has changed — that is the moment to call **Expand** and use the bigger space.

**SMALL SPACE: COMBINE. BIG SPACE: EXPLOIT.**

## Receiving posture

### Scan while the ball travels

The best time to look away is when nothing new can happen to the ball, such as while it travels between teammates.

- Look early to build the picture.
- Look again just before the ball arrives.
- Look again after your first touch.

**Quick looks are enough. You do not need to stare.**

Look for three things: your open teammate, the nearest opponent, and the space. At least one look before receiving whenever there is time; more looks are not automatically better.

### Side-on when possible

Open your hips enough to see both where the ball came from and where the next action may go.

Do not force an exaggerated side-on position if pressure or the pass angle makes another posture safer.

### Meet the ball

Do not wait flat-footed for a pass that a defender can attack.

Step toward the pass when appropriate and arrive before the defender.

**Meet the ball** applies to:

- checking to feet
- midfield receiving
- defenders intercepting
- attacking crosses and loose balls

### First touch begins the next action

A good first touch is not simply stopping the ball.

Use the arriving ball to:

- turn
- escape pressure
- carry
- protect
- set up the next pass

**Your first touch can beat a defender.**

A touch past or away from an arriving defender can take her out of the play before you pass or dribble.

**The pass tells the receiver which way to turn.** Receiving under pressure is a skill to develop, not a situation to avoid.

## Athletic posture

General principles:

- knees soft
- weight balanced
- feet alive
- not tall and locked
- ready to move in more than one direction

## 1v1 defensive posture

### Approach fast, arrive controlled

Close space quickly, then shorten the final steps so the attacker cannot beat you with one touch.

### Low, wide, and mobile

As you arrive, break down: feet a little wider than shoulder-width, knees bent, weight on the balls of your feet.

Get low and wide enough to move side to side, but do not sink so low that movement becomes slow.

### Avoid unnecessary crossing of the feet

Use controlled side-to-side and crossover steps, depending on the attacker's distance and speed.

### Show rather than dive

Use your body angle to influence the attacker toward less dangerous space.

### Delay when she has the advantage

**Protect → slow → show → wait → win.** Stay between her and goal, take away her speed, steer her away from danger, stay balanced, and win the ball when the moment comes. An excellent delay may contain no tackle. See [Coaching Game Model §21](coaching-game-model.md#21-delay-defending).

## Pursuit angles

When you are recovering, do not simply chase the attacker's back.

**Do not run to where the attacker is. Run to where she is going.**

Aim first toward the dangerous lane between the attacker and goal, then curve your recovery run as the gap closes.

This helps you:

- cut off central space
- shorten the route to goal
- force the attacker wider
- recover without needing to win a pure footrace from behind

## Movement timing

**Move on the pass. Move again after you pass.**

### Move on the pass

Anticipate the next picture while the ball is traveling. Start moving as the pass is played, not after it arrives.

### Move again after you pass

After every pass, ask:

**Where can I help next?**

### Arrive, don't camp

If you get to a useful space too early, defenders can settle and block the lane.

Move into the space as the passing lane develops.

### Different heights

Avoid standing in a flat line with nearby teammates when possible.

Staggering — some of you higher up the field, some deeper — creates angles and third-player options.

## Defensive vision

Gather information about:

- ball
- goal
- teammates
- opponents
- dangerous space

**See the ball and the most dangerous space or opponent.** Do not become so focused on a single mark that you lose the larger picture.

**Protect dangerous space first. Get tighter as the ball and danger get closer.**

Get compact first, then pick up the dangerous opponent in your part of the field. Get tight on restarts (free kicks, corners, and throw-ins), on crosses, and when you step to win the ball.

## Transition posture

Good defensive positioning should help the next attack.

Good attacking positioning should help the next defensive transition.

**Defend from a position you can attack from.**

## Final standard

Good movement should make teammates' decisions easier.

**Create an angle. Meet the ball. Stay balanced. Move again.**
